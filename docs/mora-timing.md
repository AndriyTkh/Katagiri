# Mora timing — the rhythm rule

Japanese gives **every mora equal duration**. English compresses unstressed syllables; doing that in Japanese makes you hard to understand in a way that no amount of vocabulary fixes.

## What counts as one mora

| Thing | Example | Moras |
|---|---|---|
| CV syllable | か | 1 |
| ん | ほん | 2 (ho-n) |
| long vowel | とう | 2 (to-o) |
| small っ | きって | 3 (ki-t-te) |
| small ゃゅょ | きょ | 1 |

## The ones that will catch you

| Pair | Moras | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| きて / きって | 2 / 3 | come / cut |
| おばさん / おばあさん | 4 / 5 | aunt / grandmother |
| ここ / こうこう | 2 / 4 | here / high school |
| かこ / かっこ | 2 / 3 | past / parenthesis |

Calling someone's grandmother "aunt" is a mora away in both directions.

## You already have this system — it's called Finnish quantity

Because you've studied Finnish, this is not a new phonological category for you. It's the same one, relabelled:

| Finnish | Japanese |
|---|---|
| tuli / tuuli | ここ / こうこう |
| tuli / tulli | きて / きって |
| kuka / kukka | かこ / かっこ |

っ **is** Finnish gemination. おう **is** Finnish `uu`. When these come up, that's the instruction — not a mnemonic, the same motor program. See [[l1-profile]].

Ukrainian gives you no vowel-length distinction, and English gives you stress-timing, which actively fights this. Finnish is the one to reach for.

## How to train it

Tap a beat with your finger — one tap per mora — while shadowing. Slow. Clapping おばあさん as five even beats feels absurd and is correct. Songs enforce this automatically, which is why karaoke is on the roadmap as a real training tool.

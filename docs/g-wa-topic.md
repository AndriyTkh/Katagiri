# は — the topic marker

**Written は, pronounced わ (wa).** No exceptions. This is the single most common beginner reading error.

## Shape

`[topic] は [comment]。`

- わたし**は** たなかです。 — As for me, I'm Tanaka.
- これ**は** みずです。 — As for this, it's water.

## What it actually does

は does not mean "is" and it is not a subject marker. It sets what the sentence is *about*, and everything after it is the comment on that topic. English has no direct equivalent; "as for X" is the closest, though it sounds stilted in English while は is completely ordinary in Japanese.

## Revised plan for you specifically

This is normally a multi-year fog, because English marks given/new with articles and stress and has rigid word order. You have better tools — see [[l1-profile]]:

- **Ukrainian free word order encodes theme–rheme** (тема/рема). は marks the theme. You may already have a name for the thing English speakers can't perceive.
- **Finnish word order encodes definiteness/givenness** — `Kirja on pöydällä` vs `Pöydällä on kirja` — which sits in nearly the same territory as は/が.

So instead of parking you at level 1 for months, we try the theme–rheme framing now and see if it lands. If it does, you skip the most famous plateau in Japanese. If it doesn't, we fall back to collecting examples and letting it emerge from data.

**Aspect trap to watch:** Ukrainian's obligatory perfective/imperfective will tempt you to map доконаний вид → た and недоконаний → ている. It half-works and then betrays you; ている covers progressive *and* resultant state, which Ukrainian splits differently. Log every instance in the error museum — I expect this to be one of your signature error patterns.

## Contrast function

は also marks contrast: にくは たべません。 (I don't eat *meat* — implying something else, I do.) This is why it feels slippery.

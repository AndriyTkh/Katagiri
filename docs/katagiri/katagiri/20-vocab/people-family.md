# Family

Japanese family vocabulary is **two parallel sets**, and this is not politeness decoration — using the wrong one sounds genuinely odd. One set for your own family when speaking to outsiders (humble), one for everyone else's family and for addressing your own (polite).

| | Own family, to outsiders | Others' / addressing yours |
|---|---|---|
| mother | 母（はは） | お母さん（おかあさん） |
| father | 父（ちち） | お父さん（おとうさん） |
| older sister | 姉（あね） | お姉さん（おねえさん） |
| older brother | 兄（あに） | お兄さん（おにいさん） |

## Sentences

- わたしの お母さんです。 — This is my mother. `polite` →[[g-desu-copula]]

## Notes

### お母さん（おかあさん）
**Five moras: o-ka-a-sa-n.** The doubled あ is a full extra beat. English speakers say it in four and it sounds wrong — and おばさん (aunt) vs おばあさん (grandmother) is the same trap with worse consequences. See [[35-phonology/mora-timing]].

Introducing your own mother to someone else, the correct word is 母（はは）です。 You say お母さん *to* her, or about someone else's.
